Reflection

Analyzing past systematic marginalization offers insight into how these moments in history have shaped, and continue to shape, present-day life. All of the visualizations summarizing EJScreen data on percent low income, PM25 percentile, and low life expectancy percentile show the highest values in grade D neighborhoods and lowest in grade A. This simple analysis points to the lasting impacts of historical marginalization, but it doesn’t fully capture the impact of redlining on Los Angeles County, since most of the current county area was never assigned an HOLC grade.

# A figure summarizing the percent of observations within redlined neighborhoods within each HOLC grade

# Filter to two columns I want
select_crashes <- switrs |> 
  select(POINT_X, POINT_Y, PEDESTRIAN_ACCIDENT, BICYCLE_ACCIDENT, NUMBER_KILLED)

# Drop na or change them under a different name?
select_crashes <- select_crashes |> 
  mutate(PEDESTRIAN_ACCIDENT = ifelse(is.na(PEDESTRIAN_ACCIDENT), "No accident", PEDESTRIAN_ACCIDENT)) |> 
  mutate(BICYCLE_ACCIDENT = ifelse(is.na(BICYCLE_ACCIDENT), "No accident", BICYCLE_ACCIDENT)) 

# Join datasets/ drop geometry
join_crashes_redlining <- st_join(select_crashes, mi_la) |> 
  st_drop_geometry()

# Create a percent table
crashes_redlining_percent <- join_crashes_redlining |>
  mutate(
    grade = ifelse(is.na(grade), "No HOLC Grade", grade)
  ) |> 
  count(grade) |>
  mutate(percent = (n / sum(n)) * 100)
# Visualize the percentage of bike and pedestrian crashes and fatalities within each HOLC grade
ggplot(data = crashes_redlining_percent,aes(fill = grade)) +
    geom_col(mapping = aes(x = grade,y= n))+
    scale_fill_manual(values = grade_colors, name = "Grade")+
  labs(
    title = "Observations within redlined neighborhoods",
    x = "HOLC Grades",
    y = "% bike and pedestrian accidents"
  )

Reflection

Bike and pedestrian accidents do not follow the pattern in the previous analysis, grade C areas have the highest percentage, followed by grade D areas. Assigning HOLC grades to areas with no assigned HOLC grade could help determine how much of this pattern is tied to redlining.
